AHG on Qredible: ISO day-one walkthrough (staging, Nov 20)

This walkthrough follows Alternative Horizons Group (AHG), an independent sales organization (ISO) that works with WestTown Payments, through its first day on Qredible. Qredible switches on monitoring for AHG's plan, WestTown sets what AHG is allowed to do, and AHG then watches the 81 WestTown merchants assigned to it in view-only monitoring. AHG also adds a new merchant of its own and sends it to WestTown with a note, and WestTown accepts it with the status it chooses. Every screen below was captured on staging on November 20, 2026.

Real merchant names, websites, emails and contacts are blurred. The merchant "ZZ-TEST AHG Prospect 20261120" is a test record created for this walkthrough. Click any screen to open it full size.

Setup (Qredible / WestTown)

Two switches decide what an ISO can see: the screen has to be available on the ISO's plan, and the processor has to grant the permission on the relationship. Both are needed.

1

Turn on assigned merchant monitoring for AHG's plan

Qredible staff · Q-Config
What the user does
In Q-Config, under Application Sources and plan access, a Qredible admin picks the ISO Onboarding plan (#44) and sets "Assigned merchant monitoring" to Available, then saves plan Source access.
What they see
The four ISO screens registered as Sources, each with its availability for this plan. Partner ISO relationship management stays Hidden; onboarding portfolio, the practice walkthrough and assigned merchant monitoring are Available.
Why it matters
The screen comes with the plan. No code change or deploy is needed to give an ISO the monitoring view, and it can be taken away the same way.
2

WestTown opens its relationship with AHG

WestTown · Partner Program, ISOs tab
What the user does
WestTown opens Edit relationship for Alternative Horizons Group.
What they see
The ISO owner, the kind (Retail), the relationship state (Active), and a list of permitted capabilities. Before setup, only "View onboarding portfolio" is checked.
Why it matters
The processor stays in control. An ISO gets nothing beyond what the processor checks here.
3

81 merchants are assigned to AHG

WestTown · ISOs tab
What the user does
WestTown saves the relationship and reviews the merchants assigned to AHG.
What they see
AHG shows as Retail, Active, with 81 assigned merchants. Below, each assigned merchant with its invitation, portal, MyCOA and completion status.
Why it matters
Assignment is explicit, merchant by merchant. AHG sees these 81 and no others from WestTown's book.
4

WestTown's "Merchants sent by ISOs" inbox starts empty

WestTown · ISOs tab
What the user does
Nothing yet. WestTown sees a new section under ISO relationships.
What they see
"Merchants sent by ISOs", filtered to Waiting for review, with the message "No merchants are waiting for review." The instructions explain that WestTown reviews each merchant and picks its status on accept: Active, Pending or Pre-underwriting.
Why it matters
Merchants that come from an ISO land in one review queue. Nothing joins WestTown's monitored book until WestTown accepts it.
5

WestTown grants two new permissions

WestTown · Edit relationship
What the user does
WestTown checks "View compliance monitoring" and can also check "Send merchants to processor", then confirms the review box and saves.
What they see
The same dialog, now with "Send merchants to processor" listed beside the other capabilities.
Why it matters
Monitoring and sending merchants are separate permissions. A processor can let an ISO watch without letting it submit merchants, or allow both.

AHG monitors WestTown's merchants (view only)

AHG opens We Monitor and sees WestTown's results for the 81 merchants assigned to it. It can read, search, filter and export. It cannot add notes, open cases or change anything.

6

No company selected: a clear reason, not an error

AHG · We Monitor
What the user does
Opens We Monitor before a company is selected.
What they see
A "View only" badge and one line: "Select a company before opening compliance monitoring."
Why it matters
The user knows what to do next instead of facing an empty table or an error.
7

The assigned merchants appear first

AHG · We Monitor, view only
What the user does
With AHG selected, opens We Monitor. "Assigned by" shows WestTown Payments, LLC.
What they see
The merchant rows load right away with stage, license status, product compliance, COA counts, cases and MyCOA status, paged 1 to 25 of 81. The website compliance, scans and MRI columns are still filling in.
Why it matters
AHG sees its list straight away instead of waiting for every column. The page states that these are WestTown's current results and the time they were read.
8

Website compliance, scans and MRI fill in

AHG · We Monitor, view only
What the user does
Waits a moment on the same page.
What they see
Website violations with severity, the latest website and product scan dates, and MRI risk signals with how many need review. Refresh and Export become available. There are no note, case or action controls.
Why it matters
AHG gets the same compliance picture WestTown sees for these merchants, so it can follow up with its merchants, while WestTown keeps sole control of decisions.

AHG adds its own merchants (create-only, no registry search)

AHG also has its own monitoring list for merchants it brings in. It can create new merchants, but it cannot search or pull in companies that are already on Qredible.

9

AHG's own We Monitor starts empty

AHG · Q-Monitor, We Monitor
What the user does
Opens AHG's own We Monitor.
What they see
The standard tabs (Pre-Underwriting, Pending, Active, Off-Boarding, Archived), all at 0, with Add Company and Upload Companies at the top.
Why it matters
This list is separate from the 81 WestTown merchants. What AHG adds here belongs to AHG until it is sent to a processor.
10

Add Companies is create-only

AHG · Add Companies
What the user does
Clicks Add Company.
What they see
One option, "Add new merchant", with the note that the account "adds new merchants only; it does not search companies already on the platform." New companies are added as Pending.
Why it matters
An ISO cannot browse or claim merchants that other customers already monitor on Qredible. That protects every other customer's book.
11

The new merchant is created

AHG · Add Companies
What the user does
Enters the merchant's details (test merchant ZZ-TEST AHG Prospect 20261120, Washington DC, Hemp / CBD, Distributor) and confirms.
What they see
A success message and the merchant listed under Selected Companies, saved as Pending.
Why it matters
Adding a prospect takes one short form. No Qredible staff are involved.
12

The merchant appears in AHG's Pending list

AHG · We Monitor, Pending
What the user does
Opens the Pending tab.
What they see
Pending (1) with the test merchant, its location and industry tags, Not Scanned for website and product, and an MRI result that already shows 1 of 4 factors screened.
Why it matters
AHG can start vetting a prospect on Qredible before it ever reaches the processor.

AHG sends a merchant to WestTown with a note

On AHG's ISO onboarding page, below its assigned merchants, a new section lists the merchants AHG added itself and lets it send them to WestTown for review.

13

"Send merchants to WestTown Payments, LLC"

AHG · ISO onboarding
What the user does
Scrolls past the assigned merchants, writes an optional note for the processor, and clicks Send on the test merchant.
What they see
The merchants AHG added, each with its status with WestTown (here, Not sent), a search box, a note field and a Send to processor button.
Why it matters
The ISO hands a pre-vetted merchant to the processor inside Qredible, with context, instead of by email or spreadsheet.
14

Sent: waiting for the processor

AHG · ISO onboarding
What the user does
Nothing more. AHG can Withdraw the request while it waits.
What they see
"1 merchant sent to WestTown Payments, LLC for review", and the status pill changes to Waiting for processor.
Why it matters
AHG always knows where each merchant stands with the processor.

WestTown reviews and accepts

The request lands in WestTown's inbox. WestTown chooses the status the merchant gets in its own book, and AHG keeps view-only monitoring of it.

15

The request from AHG arrives

WestTown · Merchants sent by ISOs
What the user does
Opens the ISOs tab.
What they see
The test merchant, sent by Alternative Horizons Group on 11/20/2026 with AHG's note, the work AHG has done so far (0 website scans, 0 product scans), MyCOA status, and Accept or Decline.
Why it matters
WestTown sees who sent the merchant, why, and how much vetting has been done before it decides.
16

WestTown picks the status: Active, Pending or Pre-underwriting

WestTown · Accept dialog
What the user does
Clicks Accept and chooses a status.
What they see
"Choose the status this merchant will have in your monitored merchants. The ISO keeps its view-only monitoring." with Active, Pending and Pre-underwriting.
Why it matters
The processor decides where the merchant sits in its own underwriting flow. The ISO cannot place a merchant straight into Active.
17

Accepted as Pending

WestTown · ISOs tab
What the user does
Confirms with Pending.
What they see
"ZZ-TEST AHG Prospect 20261120 accepted as Pending. It is now in your monitored merchants and stays visible to Alternative Horizons Group." The inbox is empty again.
Why it matters
One accept moves the merchant into WestTown's book, and AHG keeps watching it in view-only monitoring with no extra setup.

What's next

Not part of today's flow. These are the next pieces planned for ISOs.

  • Link to a merchant already on Qredible. When AHG's merchant is already a Qredible customer, AHG links to it instead of creating a duplicate, and sees only that merchant's published products and COAs.
  • Scan linked merchants. Running website and product scans on linked merchants, as a permission WestTown grants on the relationship.
  • AHG MyCOA invites. AHG invites its merchants to MyCOA. This comes with AHG's plan.
  • Bulk upload. Adding many new merchants at once from a file, still create-only.
  • Future
    Multiple processors and processor policy sharing. One ISO working with more than one processor, and a processor sharing its compliance policy so the ISO vets merchants against the processor's own rules.
