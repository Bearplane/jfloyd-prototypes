“Close period” snapshots the rate and net into a ledger row — later rate changes never rewrite history. Marking PAID is a bookkeeping stamp with a reference; the actual payment happens in accounting's own rails.

Why this exists

Two forces meet on one surface. First, the invite workflow — the funnel dashboard, contact resolution, three template scenarios including pre-underwriting, resend/revoke, the activation ledger — is campaign ops, squeezed into We Monitor, which was built for compliance ops. The moment the large invite campaign fires, it becomes the most-used surface in the processor app. Second, the partner program needs books: partners carry a code, income attributed to their invites owes them a share, and today that arithmetic exists nowhere — the data is fully recorded but never aggregated.

What already exists (verified against the schema)

The attribution chain is complete end-to-end today. The partner code lives on the processor-client relation, is stamped by the invite send, carries onto the subscription at signup, and every settled transaction and invoice (including refunds) hangs off that subscription. This is a reporting feature, not a data-model feature. What does not exist: a partner registry (who owns a code, their rate, payout contact), the aggregation layer, and a payout ledger. That is the whole build.

Information architecture

A new top-level Invites tab in the processor app. We Monitor keeps monitoring; Invites owns the funnel and the money. Three sub-tabs:

  • Funnel — the existing invites dashboard moved wholesale (no API rewrite), plus a Partner column and filter.
  • Affiliates — the financial view: one row per partner code, period-scoped (screen 2).
  • Templates — proposed: the invite-template management moves here so scenario templates live beside the sends that use them (open decision).

Attribution rules — written down before money moves

  1. The subscription's code is the source of truth for money. Funnel counts attribute via the invite; revenue attributes via the subscription. If they diverge, the money follows the subscription.
  2. First-touch, sticky. The code stamped at subscription creation persists for that subscription's life. Re-invites with a different code never re-attribute existing revenue.
  3. Refunds reduce the period they land in, not the period of the original charge. Closed periods stay closed.
  4. No code, no attribution — but unattributed revenue rolls up in an explicit row so the totals always reconcile to gross platform revenue.
  5. Share = net collected revenue × rate. Never MRR, never invoiced-but-unpaid.

Data model (additive, two tables)

Partner registry

pl_partner: code (unique), name, contact, share rate in basis points, status, owning processor, notes. Codes already in the wild get seeded rows at 0 bps so history is visible immediately; operators set real rates. CRUD is a super-admin surface in Q-Config.

Payout ledger

pl_partner_payout: period, net revenue, rate snapshot, share amount, status (ACCRUED → EXPORTED → PAID / VOID), external reference, operator, notes. Generated by the "Close period" action. No money movement in-platform — the ledger records what accounting pays outside, which keeps this entirely out of regulated-payments territory.

Kill switches

invites_tab_enabled — the tab split itself; off means invites stay inside We Monitor, revertible within a minute. affiliate_financials_enabled — the Affiliates view and partner endpoints. Both follow the platform's config-param pattern (fail closed, 60-second cache).

Open decisions (sign-off needed)

1 · Share-rate shapeFlat basis points per partner (spec'd), or tiered/per-plan? Flat ships in F-2; tiered is a later registry column, not a blocker.
2 · Who closes periods and marks PAIDAnd does accounting want the XLSX export, or an emailed statement?
3 · Retroactive attributionDo historic settled transactions on already-coded subscriptions count toward share, or does the program start at a cut date? (Spec assumes a cut date at flag-on; the data supports either.)
4 · PopulationLaunch scoped to the one processor running the program, or platform-generic from day one? Schema supports both.
5 · Templates locationMove invite-template management into the Invites tab (recommended), or leave it where it is?
6 · Naming“Affiliates” vs “Partners” in the UI. The data model says partner_code.

Explicitly out of scope

  • Any in-platform money movement to partners (ledger is bookkeeping only).
  • Multi-touch attribution — first-touch sticky only.
  • A partner self-service portal (worth a future phase, not this spec).
  • Any change to invite-sending mechanics or contact resolution.
