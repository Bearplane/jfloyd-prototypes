MyCOA Automatic Tier-Up

MyCOA plans are tiered by product count, but a merchant is slotted into a tier once, at purchase — nothing moves them afterward. This pack lays out the eight decisions the commercial side needs to make before engineering builds automatic tier movement, with mockups of every surface each decision touches. Answer (or edit) the eight calls at the bottom and engineering starts the same day.

Two features hiding in “auto tier-up”

A · Tier-at-conversion
Small, ready to build. When a trial converts to paid, charge the tier matching the count on conversion day — the first real charge is simply honest. No mid-cycle change ever happens. A platform kill switch (trial_auto_tier_enabled) is already reserved and ships dark.
B · Mid-subscription tier-up
The one needing these decisions. An active, paying subscription moves up because the merchant grew. This changes what an existing customer is billed on a stored card — every question below is about doing that without surprising anyone.
Decision 1

Which product count triggers it?

Tiers are defined by product caps, but “product count” has more than one honest answer — and the choice moves real money. On measured Westtown data, tiering on all parent products overstates the billable count by roughly 79% versus cannabis-only parents (only cannabis products require a COA).

Decision 2

When is the threshold “crossed”?

OptionImplication
Sustained above the cap for N days (e.g. 14)A bulk upload that gets trimmed the next day never triggers anything. Protects trust; slightly delays revenue.Recommended
The instant the count exceeds the capMaximum revenue capture; punishes normal catalog housekeeping and invites support tickets.Your call
Decision 3

Up only — or down too?

OptionImplication
Up only; downgrades via supportNo gaming incentive (archive products before renewal, restore after). Revenue never silently gives itself back.Recommended
Automatic both directions“Fairest” on paper; creates an archive-before-billing-date exploit and noisy tier churn.Not advised
Decision 4

Notify-first, or change the bill immediately?

The customer-experience call, and the biggest engineering fork. Notify + apply at next renewal reuses billing machinery that already exists (the renewal simply charges the new tier; the notice rides the existing pre-billing notice system). Immediate mid-cycle proration is a brand-new payment path — partial charges, failed-charge handling, credit notes.

Decision 5

What about annual plans?

The biggest revenue-timing question. A merchant who up-tiers in month 2 of an annual plan either waits ~10 months at the old price, gets a mid-year prorated charge, or gets switched to monthly at the new tier.

OptionImplication
Wait for the annual renewal (notice at renewal time)Simplest and matches the v1 mechanism; revenue lag can be most of a year.Recommended v1
Prorate mid-yearNeeds the full proration path from Decision 4’s later phase — plus credit-note handling.Later phase
Switch to monthly at the new tierPunishes annual commitment; effectively a plan change the merchant didn’t ask for.Not advised
Decision 6

If the higher charge fails?

Only bites if you pick an immediate/prorated path — under notify-at-renewal, a failed charge is just the normal renewal-failure flow at the new price.

OptionImplication
Normal payment-failed flow at the new tierOne consistent dunning story; the tier change stands.Recommended
Revert to the old tier and retry laterTwo sources of truth for what the merchant owes; messy support conversations.Not advised
Decision 7

Exceptions — can staff pin a company’s tier?

Enterprise and negotiated deals shouldn’t be touched by an algorithm. We’d default this to yes: a per-company override in Q-Config that auto-tier-up never crosses.

Decision 8

Who writes the notice email?

The notice can be fixed Qredible copy, or a customer-editable card on the existing My Templates page — the same machinery the MyCOA activation invitation just joined (QP-4415), with tokens and fallbacks.
